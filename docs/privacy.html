crazy-streamer-toolbox

Privacy policy

Last changed 2026-11-27. It describes the newest version of the toolbox.

crazy-streamer-toolbox is a program that runs on your own computer. It has no server of its own and its makers receive nothing from it: no account, no analytics, no telemetry. It only talks to a service or program when you connect it, or for the few things listed under What goes online without a connection.

What it keeps

Everything the toolbox keeps (settings, logins, points, flows, widgets, alerts) stays in its own config folder on your computer. Logins, tokens, keys and passwords for connected services are kept in config/secrets/, never in the settings file and never shown on screen or written into a log. You can delete that folder at any time.

Opening the toolbox from other devices

By default only your own computer can open the toolbox's pages. If you switch on access from other devices on your network, they must sign in with a password you set; the password is stored only as a hash.

Connections

Every connection is off until you switch it on. Once on, it talks only to that service or program, with the login, key or token you gave it, to do what your flows ask. What it receives is used only on your computer and is not sent anywhere else, sold or shared. Using a service through the toolbox also falls under that service's own terms and privacy policy.

Streaming platforms

YouTube (Google). You log in with a Google Cloud app of your own. The toolbox then reads your channel name, your live broadcasts, their live chat (messages, Super Chats, Super Stickers, memberships) and your newest public subscribers. If you switch on "May write in chat", it also sends the chat messages your flows write. You can remove the access at any time under Connections → YouTube or at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

The toolbox's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. See also the YouTube Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy.

Twitch. You log in through the toolbox's own Twitch app by confirming a short code on Twitch's page. The login is kept on your computer only; the toolbox's makers never receive it or anything it reads. The toolbox reads your channel's events (chat, follows, subscriptions, gifts, Bits, channel point redemptions, raids, ad breaks, stream online / offline) and does what your flows ask: send chat messages, change the stream title or category, manage channel point rewards, set stream markers and, only if you allow the extra permissions, things like clips, polls, predictions, raids, shoutouts, announcements and moderation. You can remove the access at any time under Connections → Twitch or at twitch.tv/settings/connections.

Rumble. Uses the Live Stream API address from your Rumble account (it holds your key). Reads follows, subscribers, gifted subs, and while you are live your chat and rants. Read only.

Fansly. Joins the chatroom whose id you type, the way Fansly's own website does, and reads its chat, tips and subscriptions. Read only; no login and nothing of your account is used.

Tips, donations and alerts services

StreamElements and Streamlabs. With your account's token, the toolbox listens to your activity feed (tips, donations, store redemptions, merch, charity, test alerts, and for Streamlabs the events of the platforms linked to it). Listening only; nothing on the account is changed. If you import a StreamElements widget or overlay, the toolbox downloads it from StreamElements (and the fonts it uses from Google Fonts).

Tiltify. With a Tiltify app of your own, reads the new donations to the campaign you choose.

Crowd Control. Listens to your public Crowd Control feed (the effects viewers buy). No login.

StreamSocket Events (Twitch extension). Listens for the extension's button presses on your channel, with the secret from its configuration page.

Heart rate

Pulsoid and HypeRate. Read your heart rate from your own Pulsoid or HypeRate account so flows and widgets can show it. The toolbox keeps only the latest value, in memory; it goes nowhere else unless a flow you build sends it.

Messages to other places

Discord. Posts the messages your flows write into one channel, through that channel's webhook. Nothing is read from Discord.

Web request boxes. Boxes made of JSON (by you or a plugin) call the web address they name, with the values your flow gives them. Where they go is shown on the box.

Programs on your own computer

OBS, VTube Studio, Streamer.bot, Mix It Up, SAMMI, Firebot, Lumia Stream, Voicemod, veadotube, VNyan, Warudo, T.I.T.S. These talk to programs running on your computer (or your local network), not to the internet. Any token or password they need is kept in config/secrets/. Streamer.bot and Lumia Stream can pass on events from platforms they are connected to themselves.

Keyboard triggers. While switched on, the toolbox notices key presses on this computer so a key (for example F23 from a Stream Deck) can start a flow. Only the keys your flows ask for are used; every other key press is dropped at once. Typed text is never kept, shown or logged.

What goes online without a connection

  • Update check. When it starts and every 6 hours while it runs, the toolbox asks GitHub's public API for this project's newest release. An update is only downloaded and installed when you say so.
  • Emotes in widgets. When a chat widget needs your channel's BetterTTV and FrankerFaceZ emotes, the toolbox asks BetterTTV and FrankerFaceZ for them by your Twitch channel id. Twitch emote images come from Twitch.
  • Twitch clip downloads. The first time a flow downloads a Twitch clip, the toolbox gets the free program TwitchDownloader from its GitHub releases (checked against its SHA-256) and looks for a newer one at most once a day.
  • Plugins you install are made by other people and can do anything the toolbox can. Their privacy is up to them; read what a plugin does before you install it.

These services see your computer's IP address, like any website you visit.

Changes

When the toolbox starts using data in a new way, this page changes with it. Its history is in the project's git history.
